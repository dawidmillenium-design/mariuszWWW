# SHADOW ROUTES

## The Documentary That Becomes a Computer Game — Complete Scenario in Seven Episodes

[SCENE START]

[VISUAL]
A satellite view of Earth at night. The glowing webs of civilization pulse against the dark. A neon-green line ignites in the jungles of Southeast Asia, snakes west across the Indian Ocean, hooks through a rain-black port in Georgia, crosses Poland on eight-lane asphalt, dives into the container maze of Rotterdam, and vanishes west over the Atlantic.

[AUDIO — KENJI ARAMAKI, NARRATOR]
For decades the drug trade ran on a predictable, violent rhythm. Heroin from the Golden Crescent. Cocaine from the Andean ridge. We thought we understood the currency of the underworld. Then, in 2023, informants on three continents started whispering about a new product. Brilliantly colored. Absurdly valuable. And here is the part that kept analysts awake: nobody was getting high.

[VISUAL]

[AUDIO — KENJI]

Episodes Six and Seven are a game. The save file does not care which one you came for.

TITLE CARD: SHADOW ROUTES.

## THE FOUR

YAYAYAhujUS — "The Laughing Node." Real name unknown; the alias is his own invention and his own laugh, three bright syllables stapled to a word that three Slavic languages consider an insult and that Interpol stenographers record as "Exhibit Y." Expelled from the Yakuza for reasons his former brothers refuse to describe on record, he rebuilt himself in the borderlands of Vietnam and Cambodia as the extraction chief of the Emerald Compound. He negotiates with warlords and cartel killers in penthouse suites, and he wins with jokes. Watch the laugh closely. By Episode Four you will hear what it costs him.

MARIUSZ "WWW" — "The Iron Fleet." Mariusz Wieslaw Wojtowicz: three given names, three Ws, one logistics empire run out of truck stops near Białystok, Poland. His trucks wear the livery WWW LOGISTICS, which in the twenty-first century reads as a harmless tech firm; Europol's own mail server once quarantined three task-force reports about him as spam. He does not joke. He does not raise his voice. He moves anything, anywhere, inside the boring machinery of ordinary freight, and he considers YAYAYAhujUS personally responsible for everything unserious about the criminal profession.

KENJI ARAMAKI — "The Investigator." Japanese documentary journalist, our narrator and on-camera presence. His network paid for a drug-war film with a clean ending. He kept handing them interviews in which no drug ever quite appears. In Episode Six, he becomes playable.

BLACKHUJUS — "The Black Sovereign." The apex. Never filmed, never met, never pronounced aloud inside any agency that respects its own insurance policies. He holds the chemical markets of Africa, the enforcement crews of the Americas, and the laundries of Rio de Janeiro. His subordinates never say his name; they say Downstairs, meaning the man and the project at once. Investigators, when they finally understood, gave it a colder name: the Hollow Republic.

## EPISODE ONE — THE LAUGHING NODE

[VISUAL]
Helicopter footage over the Vietnam–Cambodia border. Monsoon rain flattens the canopy. Beneath the foliage, an industrial encampment buzzes around the clock: excavators, chemical drums, armed men in plastic ponchos.

[AUDIO — DR. ELIAS VANCE, FORMER INTERPOL STRATEGIC ANALYST]
It appeared out of nowhere. 2023. A green powder with a street value that made pure cocaine look like table salt. But we intercepted tons of it and never found a single user. No overdoses. No dealers. No informants. It moved across the planet like a rumor with a shipping schedule.

[VISUAL]
Surveillance footage, Bangkok. On a penthouse terrace above the neon, a man in an immaculate white suit holds court over two delegations who were, ten minutes ago, pointing guns at each other across his table. Everyone is laughing. One African buyer is wiping his eyes. An American cartel man pounds the table in delight.

[AUDIO — SPECIAL AGENT "ECHO," ASEANAPOL UNDERCOVER]
We called him the Joker before we knew the alias. Buyers would fly in ready to kill him over pricing. He would pour whiskey, lean in, and go, "Ya! Ya! Ya!" — that laugh, always three, always before the punchline — and tell some filthy, devastating joke in broken English, and within five minutes these men were signing bearer bonds. I watched him settle a four-million-dollar dispute with a joke about a goat and a customs officer I cannot repeat on camera. Humor was his weapon. It disarmed everyone. That should have frightened us more than it did.

[AUDIO — KENJI]
Why the Yakuza expelled him is the one question nobody answers twice. Three former associates gave me three versions: a casino, a debt, a funeral. A fourth only shook his head. "He laughed at the wrong funeral," he said. "In our world you can steal. You can kill. You do not make the widow laugh." I kept all four; a man that funny needed an origin story, and the underworld wrote several.

[AUDIO — KENJI, ON CAMERA]
His genius was misdirection. He flooded Pattaya with dummy shipments, complete with real street drugs, so Thai narcotics police could score televised seizures on a reliable schedule. He fed them victories like a man feeding ducks. Meanwhile the actual Emerald Compound rolled to the deep-water port at night, in unmarked containers, and slipped into the South China Sea.

[VISUAL]
Covert footage at the border checkpoint. A guard waves a truck through without opening it. The driver tosses something small and bright. Close-up, later: a casino chip from the Lucky Pearl, a Pattaya casino that burned down in 2019. Remember this chip.

[AUDIO — KENJI]
Every route needs keys. On the Asian leg, the keys were casino chips from a casino that no longer existed. Untraceable, worthless elsewhere, and redeemable at one cage window in Bangkok. We found that out much later. We found everything out much later.

EYES ONLY — DEEP COVER INTERCEPT, AGENT "ECHO"

Static. Heavy breathing. "Command, this is Echo. I'm inside the primary extraction zone. It's not a drug lab. Repeat: it is not a narcotic. The Emerald Compound is a hyper-corrosive chemical solvent. It melts bedrock. It turns granite to slag and the seismographs don't even twitch. They aren't farming drugs here. They are digging." The transmission ends mid-word.

[AUDIO — KENJI]
That file sat mislabeled on an evidence server for eight months. Which brings us to the route.

## EPISODE TWO — THE GREEN POWDER TRAIL

[VISUAL]
Pattaya at night: tourist neon, police lights, a televised seizure of a speedboat stacked with green-branded bricks. Cut to forty kilometers up the coast, where a container ship slides out of Laem Chabang without lights worth mentioning.

[AUDIO — DR. VANCE]
The chemistry defied us. The powder was heavy. It smelled of ozone and burning copper. When our lab ran certain electrical frequencies across a sample, it glowed. Actual, visible light. We had four national laboratories working on it, and the most authoritative sentence anyone produced was, and I quote: "We have no idea, but it is not for snorting."

[VISUAL]
The Indian Ocean, from the deck of the ship. A night storm. Below deck, in the camera's night-vision green, the stacked containers pulse faintly each time the radar sweeps overhead. The crew crosses themselves and looks away.

[AUDIO — "BOATSWAIN," CREW MEMBER, VOICE DISGUISED]
Twelve days to Chittagong. When the radar swept us the containers hummed, and under the hum was that light, green like a bottle's bottom. The cook said it was the refrigeration. Refrigeration does not glow. I slept with the lamp on and counted my pay twice, once for me, once for whoever was listening. I have moved some things in my life, my friend. That cargo was the only one that ever felt awake.

[AUDIO — KENJI]
The route was a logistical miracle with a criminal's patience. Chittagong in Bangladesh, where port officials were paid in bearer bonds. Overland through India in convoys indistinguishable from ten thousand other convoys. Then the mountains of Iran, where the bribes changed currency but not temperature. Hostile borders, bridged with cash: everyone on the line got a small, deniable, excellent reason not to look.

[VISUAL]
Kenji on camera in a Bangkok police colonel's office. The wall behind the colonel is dense with medals and framed seizure photographs, every brick in every photo faintly green.

[AUDIO — COLONEL CHALERM, ROYAL THAI POLICE]
We seized record quantities. Record. You can check. This office made the Green Powder Trail a priority target and we were extremely successful.

[AUDIO — KENJI, VOICEOVER]
Every brick he ever seized was a decoy with a street value of roughly the frame it is now hanging in. His career is a museum of YAYAYAhujUS's stage management. He still does not know. We decided not to tell him. Some interviews you do for information; some you do so the audience understands what being played looks like.

[AUDIO — DR. VANCE]
By 2025 the math was breaking down. The money exceeded the GDP of small countries, and the expenditures made no sense. Not yachts. Not politicians. Heavy metallurgy. Geological surveys. We seized a purchase order in Rio for forty kilometers of industrial conveyor belt, delivery address in a country with no mines. I remember reading it twice and feeling the floor of my career tilt.

[VISUAL]
The purchase order fills the screen. The delivery coordinates blink once.

[AUDIO — KENJI]
The trail led west. It always led west. And at the western edge of the old world, in the rain, the cargo changed hands.

## EPISODE THREE — THE EYE OF THE NEEDLE

[VISUAL]
A drone crosses the Black Sea toward Batumi, Georgia. Rain falls in sheets over a city where crumbling Soviet brutalism leans against brand-new glass towers. Port cranes loom in the mist like grazing iron animals.

[AUDIO — KENJI]
The Asian and Middle Eastern networks could only push the product so far. To enter Europe, the Emerald Compound had to pass through the eye of the needle. Batumi: a booming port with a foot in the old world and one in the new, and a practiced habit of not asking cargo where it had been.

[VISUAL]
Covert dashcam footage. Transport trucks idle in a rain-slicked shipyard. Men in heavy coats exchange thick manila envelopes, then share tea from a thermos, unhurried, almost ceremonial. On a container door, stenciled: WWW LOGISTICS.

[AUDIO — DR. VANCE]
We knew him only as Marek, later as Mariusz. The alias WWW was a gift to him from the alphabet and a curse to us, because in the twenty-first century a truck that says WWW is invisible. You see a hundred a day and your brain files them under "internet." His underworld handle, the unprintable one, was a Polish vulgarity that told you everything about his manners. He was a former truck driver who became the linchpin of European logistics. If you wanted to move a tank from Kyiv to Paris without a single camera noticing, you called him. He was not a gangster in a tailored suit. He was a schedule with a grudge.

[VISUAL]
Kenji interviews a round, cheerful man in a truck wash outside Białystok. A sign reads WALDEMAR'S. Steam everywhere.

[AUDIO — WALDEMAR WOJTOWICZ, COUSIN OF THE SUBJECT]
Mariusz? Best driver in the family. Worst wedding guest. At my wedding he danced once. Once. We have a photograph; scientists study it. You ask was he a serious boy? At age nine he organized the other children's toy cars by axle weight. Axle weight! The other mothers were frightened. (He laughs, then stops.) You will not find him. Nobody finds Mariusz. Mariusz finds you, usually with an invoice.

[VISUAL]
The shipyard, night. The handoff itself: YAYAYAhujUS's Bangkok lieutenant, all gold rings and good cheer, steps from a car and opens his arms wide to Mariusz, who stands motionless under an umbrella held by a subordinate. The lieutenant says something. The rings gesture broadly. Mariusz does not move. The lieutenant's smile dries up. The cargo changes custody in total silence.

[AUDIO — SPECIAL AGENT "GHOST," EUROPOL UNDERCOVER, VOICE DISGUISED]
There is a rule on that route: you do not joke with Mariusz. Ping's people, sorry, YAYAYAhujUS's people, they are all comedians, they think fear is a negotiation. Mariusz thinks a joke is a paperwork error waiting to happen. That friction nearly killed the whole pipeline, and one typed joke came closer than five years of our surveillance ever did.

[AUDIO — KENJI]
From Batumi, the convoy rolled west through the Caucasus corridor, past the war's edge, toward the most monitored border in Europe. Inside the trucks, the green powder rode in a new disguise: packed tight into hollowed engine blocks, generator casings, agricultural machinery. Hidden where heat was supposed to be.

## EPISODE FOUR — THE IRON FLEET

[VISUAL]
A truck stop near Białystok at 4 a.m. Sodium lights, diesel fog, rows of idling semis. Inside a garage, welders seal heavy cast-iron casings. Sparks fall on green dust and it flares briefly, magnesium-bright. The welders do not react. They have seen it before.

[AUDIO — KENJI]
Mariusz ran an empire built on diesel exhaust, cheap coffee, and extreme paranoia. Europol considered the Polish-Ukrainian border the most watched stretch of land on the continent. Mariusz moved tons across it anyway, in leased ghost vehicles registered to defunct shell companies in the Baltics, bribing border guards with crypto sent to hardware wallets passed in the backs of kebab shops.

[AUDIO — AGENT "GHOST"]
I rode that corridor for five months as a driver. The trick was never speed. The trick was boredom. His convoys were the dullest vehicles on any road: correct weight, correct paperwork, drivers who stopped for soup on schedule. Thermal cameras saw engine blocks being warm, which is what engine blocks do. I watched a customs dog walk over a million dollars' worth of the compound and ask for a treat.

[AUDIO — AGENT "GHOST"]
The crossing once took eleven minutes. The guard checked the seals, checked his phone, checked the seals again, and waved us through. His new roof had been paid in a cryptocurrency he could not spell, into a wallet he could not open. That was Mariusz's real talent. He did not buy silence. He bought people into paperwork they could never explain.

[VISUAL]
A manifest page fills the screen. Under "Cargo description," in English: INDUSTRIAL GENERATORS. Under "Handling notes," someone has typed: "Fragile. Contents: one (1) Polish sense of humor. Do not drop."

[AUDIO — AGENT "GHOST"]
And then Bangkok did the stupidest thing I have ever seen a professional organization do. YAYAYAhujUS hid a joke in the manifest. A handling note, making fun of Mariusz. Typed. In the document. A Warsaw customs clerk read it, laughed, and looked twice at a load she would normally wave through. We were forty minutes from losing the entire European corridor because the funniest man in Asia could not resist a bit.

## EPISODE FIVE — BERTH 44

[VISUAL]
Rotterdam at dawn. A Europol strike team cuts open a suspect container on Berth 44. Inside: tractors. Only tractors. The team leader stands very still, holding a manifest that insists otherwise.

[AUDIO — DUTCH CUSTOMS INSPECTOR, ANONYMOUS]
We scan maybe two percent. On a good day, with coffee, two and a half. His people owned the crane schedules, the weighbridge software, the manifest layer. The cargo we hunted was never in the container we opened. It was in the container three rows over that the computer said had already left.

[AUDIO — KENJI]
From Rotterdam, Boston. From Boston, the money had to become clean, and for that the operation used Rio de Janeiro: never-occupied luxury towers, art auctions where buyer and seller wore the same employer's watch, banking corridors washing billions back to Africa and Asia.

[AUDIO — KENJI, VOICEOVER]
Boston was never a market. It was an invoice. The containers arrived nearly empty, declared their value, generated their paper, and the paper went south to be washed. What America imported was documentation. The tunnels were being financed with the one thing no scanner can see inside: arithmetic.

[VISUAL]
A hotel room in Rio. A man filmed from behind a frosted panel, voice flattened by a modulator.

[AUDIO — FORMER CARTEL LIEUTENANT]
We called him the Imperator. Some said the Black Sovereign. The alias, the ugly one, nobody said twice. I never met him. Nobody I know met him. If Ping was the brain and Mariusz was the muscle, he was the weather. You do not negotiate with weather. You meet your quotas and you pray it stays sunny.

## EPISODE SIX — PRESS START

[VISUAL]
The menu breathes. Behind it, the evidence map from the documentary: the same nodes Kenji pinned for five episodes. Jungle camp. Bangkok. Pattaya. Chittagong. Batumi. Białystok. Warsaw. Berth 44. Boston. Rio. Strings that held photographs now hold routes, and every route carries a state tag: OPEN, MONITORED, BURNED, TUNNELING.

[AUDIO — KENJI, VOICEOVER]
We released the map because a film can only show you a system; it cannot make you complicit in one. This is Operation Shadow Routes, the playable version of everything you just watched. The documentary you sat through was the tutorial. The save file remembers it.

CLASSIFIED INTELLIGENCE DOSSIER — OPERATION: SHADOW ROUTES — THREAT LEVEL: CRITICAL

CAMPAIGN ONE — THE OPERATOR. You run the machine. Three nodes, three playstyles, one payroll. As YAYAYAhujUS in Asia, extraction is a people business: keep crews laughing, keep buyers charmed, and schedule decoy seizures so the task force wins often enough to stay funded and never enough to get lucky. Social manipulation is your resource tree; every joke is a dice roll with a body count attached. As Mariusz in Europe, the game becomes logistics and risk: ghost fleets, falsified manifests, crypto bribes, and a Heat meter that climbs every time anyone, anywhere, does anything memorable. As BLACKHUJUS's viceroy in the endgame, the camera pulls out to the whole planet: laundries in Boston and Rio, suppression of rival cartels, governments bought by the province. Your drill at the Mexico–USA artery starts at 94 percent. Finish it.

CAMPAIGN TWO — THE INVESTIGATOR. You play Kenji. Same map, opposite direction. You cannot shut down a route you cannot prove, so you build cases: interview the bench of witnesses from the film — Vance, Echo, Ghost, Omega, the colonel, the cousin, the pit boss — and assemble what they give you into chains strong enough to flip a node from OPEN to BURNED before the Operator flips it to TUNNELING and takes it off the board forever. Your enemies are not soldiers. They are deadlines, lawyers, and doubt.

THE FACTION REPUTATION SYSTEM. Four factions watch everything both campaigns do. The Sovereignty pays you or buries you. The Joint Task Force lends you surveillance assets or raids your sources. The Dockside — unions, fixers, border guards, crane schedulers — opens doors for whoever kept them fed. The Fourth Estate decides whether your evidence becomes history or one evening's outrage. Reputation is currency and armor both: high Dockside standing gets the Operator into Berth 44 without a bribe; high Fourth Estate standing lets the Investigator publish with one source instead of three. Every choice moves at least two gauges, always in opposite directions. There are no free favors. There were none in the film either.

EVIDENCE VERIFICATION. Every clue arrives as a card with provenance: who touched it, when, and why they might be lying. The Sovereignty seeds the map with canaries — forgeries good enough to pass one check and wrong in exactly one detail, planted to see who acts on them. Publish a canary unverified, and a source burns: arrested, disappeared, or turned. The Lucky Pearl chip is the tutorial case. Its provenance lists a casino that burned in 2019. Catch that, and the guard's whole chain opens. Miss it, and he laughs at you from behind his pit for the rest of the run. Audio File #44-B, the Bangkok wiretap, is real, but its laugh cadence has been spliced; a careful ear proves the room held four men, not three, and the fourth man changes the ending you can reach.

THE EVOLVING WORLD MAP. Both campaigns play the same persistent world, in the same save if you dare. What the Operator builds, the Investigator raids. A corridor left MONITORED in one session greets the other campaign with cameras already bolted to it. Routes age: neglect a bribed checkpoint and it goes MONITORED on its own; overuse one and it goes BURNED and takes a witness with it. The map also keeps the film's continuity. If, as the Operator, you paid the border guard in Episode One with casino chips, that choice is already in your Investigator's evidence pool, glowing, waiting. If you spared a courier in Episode Two instead of making an example of him, he is available to testify in Episode Five. If you let YAYAYAhujUS put the joke in Mariusz's manifest, Dockside suspicion of WWW Logistics stays permanently elevated, because the Dockside never forgets being embarrassed. Choices are not flagged as important when you make them. That is the point. They were not flagged in real life either.

The map also keeps a score you cannot see. Every verified chain, every burned source, every joke deployed at the wrong checkpoint feeds a hidden ledger that the endgame reads back on one cold summary screen: what you built, what it cost, and who is no longer available for interview because of when you chose to be clever. Players who want a clean conscience can start over; players who want the truth will find it cheaper the second time.

## EPISODE SEVEN — FOUR WAYS DOWN

[VISUAL]
The endgame screen. One artery, Mexico to the USA, one drill head, one percentage climbing toward a hundred. Both campaigns converge on the same countdown. Underneath the interface, barely audible: the hum.

[AUDIO — KENJI, VOICEOVER]
The documentary had one ending I could not film, because it had not happened yet. So the game has four. Three you can steer toward. One steers toward you. They are not ranked; the documentary has already filmed pieces of all of them.

ENDING ONE — CONCRETE DAWN. The Operator wins. The drill breaks through, the Hollow Republic opens for business, and the final shot is the cold open played backward: the Earth at night, glowing as ever, while underneath it the red lines light up one by one until the surface map looks like the decoration it always was. Achievement unlocked: DOWNSTAIRS. Over the credits, a shipping forecast read in a calm voice, listing ports that no longer matter.

ENDING TWO — BERTH 44. The Investigator wins. One verified chain, complete to the Sovereignty ledger, published with Fourth Estate backing, triggers simultaneous raids in nine countries at one synchronized hour. Warehouses, garages, the Bangkok penthouse, the truck stop. YAYAYAhujUS is arrested mid-joke; the arresting officers are laughing before they read him his rights, in its way his final victory. The Rio penthouse is empty. On the floor sits a single shipping crate, addressed to Kenji, return address one word: Downstairs. Inside: a hard hat, his size.

ENDING THREE — THE TURNCOAT. Reachable only if Mariusz's one human loophole survived both campaigns: the cousin, the truck wash, the wedding photograph. Offered a choice between the Sovereign's silence and his family's visibility, Mariusz parks his entire fleet across four borders at noon, engines running, doors open. The map splits. Some tunnels are sealed with their crews still inside; the game does not show this and does not need to. He enters witness protection as the anonymous owner of a truck stop on a road you have already driven. Final shot: Mariusz watching television alone, and at something on the screen — we never learn what — he laughs. Once. Scientists study the recording.

ENDING FOUR — THE HOLLOW WORLD. The hidden ending, and the true one. Verify everything, including the things the game insists are settled, and the evidence stops cooperating: the concrete in the Mexico artery cured years before Kenji ever heard of green powder. The interviews restage themselves; a boom mic is visible in the Bangkok glass. The documentary was financed, through six shells, by the Sovereignty. The game you are playing is a screening instrument, measuring who builds well and who snitches well. Every save file phones home. Final menu, no options, white text on black: THANK YOU FOR APPLYING.

[AUDIO — KENJI]
Whichever road you took, the film ends the same way, because the film is mine. My broadcast aired the verified core: the solvent, the machines, the ledgers, the maps. Four governments denied everything in four languages within one hour, which careful viewers will notice is faster than four governments have ever done anything. The task force sealed the evidence. The public argued for a summer and moved on. And in Białystok, at the truck wash, Waldemar still washes trucks, and sometimes, he told me, the drivers pay with casino chips. (Beat.) The documentary is over. The routes are not. They were never the story. The story is how long it took us to look down.
